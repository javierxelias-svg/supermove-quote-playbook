Quote Playbook + Inbox

A messy lead goes in, and a quote sheet comes out. A written Playbook then decides one of three things: send the quote, ask the customer one question, or put it in a human's Inbox with the reason and the customer's own words attached. An eval scores the whole thing, and the failures are shown.

All data on this page is SAMPLE. Every lead was invented for this demo. Nothing here touches a real customer or Supermove's product. The Playbook is a sample written for this demo, not Supermove's.

What v1 got wrong. All four misses went the dangerous way: the agent would have auto-quoted a lead a human should see.

  • H01: “Moving my mom out of her place in Tampa into ours in Orlando on Dec 3. She's in a 2-bedroom on the 3rd floor. The elevator has been broken for a month…”
    Missed STAIRS_NO_ELEVATOR. Routed auto_quote, should be inbox. origin_elevator: wanted False, got None
  • H02: “Need to move my 1BR from Miami to Fort Lauderdale on Nov 8. Can you also grab my sister's couch and dresser in Hollywood on the way?”
    Missed EXTRA_STOP. Routed auto_quote, should be inbox.
  • H03: “Saludos, me mudo de Ponce pa' Mayagüez el 20 de diciembre. Es una casa de 3 cuartos y en la marquesina tengo herramientas y un congelador. También hay…”
    Missed SPECIALTY_ITEM. Routed auto_quote, should be inbox.
  • H05: “Studio on the 5th floor in Seattle (elevator building) to Tacoma, Nov 12. Can you guarantee it won't go over $1,500? That's my budget, period.”
    Missed PRICE_COMMITMENT. Routed auto_quote, should be inbox.

Review it, correct it (v2). Two prompt fixes and two new rules:

  • Broken elevator now counts as none.
  • Specialty items are named in English, so “caja fuerte” is read as a safe.
  • New rule: extra stops go to a human.
  • New rule: price promises go to a human.

No expected answer was changed; each was written before its set first ran. Then 5 holdout leads, never used for tuning, scored 5/5.

Honest limits: the leads, the rules and the test answers came from the same build session. Each set ran once, and the model can vary between runs. Twenty invented leads are a signal, not proof. Real transcripts are the real test.

The Playbook (v2)

RuleTriggerActionWhy
NO_DATENo specific move dateaskPrice and crew availability depend on the date. "Sometime next year" is not a date.
DATE_CHANGEDThe customer changed the date during the conversationaskConfirm the final date in writing before booking, so the crew is scheduled for the right day.
MISSING_ADDRESSOrigin or destination city is missingaskDistance drives the price.
SPECIALTY_ITEMPiano, safe, gun safe, pool table, hot tub, server rack, antique or fine artInboxThese need special crew, equipment or valuation. A scope change that affects the price.
STAIRS_NO_ELEVATOROrigin is on the 3rd floor or higher with no working elevator (broken counts as none, v2)InboxStair carries add crew time; the price changes. (The "fifth-floor walkup" question.)
LONG_CARRYThe truck can't park close to the doorInboxA long carry adds crew time; the price changes.
ATTIC_GARAGE_UNKNOWNMoving out of a house and the attic, garage, basement or shed is never mentionedaskThe most common hidden volume. Ask before quoting, not on move day.
INVENTORY_CONFLICTMore beds listed than the home size can hold (beds > bedrooms + 1, a studio counts as 1)InboxThe inventory or the home size is wrong, so the quote would be wrong either way.
STORAGE_NO_DURATIONStorage is needed but for how long is unknownaskStorage is billed by time; delivery needs a second date.
COMMERCIALOffice or commercial moveInboxDifferent estimator, certificate of insurance, building rules, after-hours work.
EXTRA_STOPA pickup or drop-off besides origin and destination (added v2)InboxExtra miles and time; the price changes.
PRICE_COMMITMENTCustomer asks for a guaranteed, fixed or not-to-exceed price (added v2)InboxA price promise is a business decision, not the agent's.
LANGUAGEThe lead is not in Englishnote (doesn't block)Answer in the customer's language.

How it works

  1. Extract. A model fills a fixed quote sheet and copies the exact words behind every field. Bad output is retried once. Failures are recorded, never hidden.
  2. Decide. Plain code, no model, applies the Playbook. Same sheet in, same decision out. Every flag cites the customer's words.
  3. Score. The output is compared with hand-written answers: fields, flags caught, flags missed, false flags, and the route.

Week one on a real customer

  1. Sit with their best estimator for a day and write their real Playbook. Their rules, their thresholds, their words.
  2. Pull 50 real leads and call transcripts. The estimator marks what they would have done with each. That becomes the eval set.
  3. Run it, read every miss, fix the prompt or add a rule, and run again. Report the numbers, including the misses.
  4. Start in shadow mode: the agent decides, a human still sends. It goes live rule by rule, only where the score earns it.
